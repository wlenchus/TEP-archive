## 1. Theorem A (single-object reduction; unconditional)

Exactly one of: (i) L(s,ρ̃) has no poles, and ρ̃ is automorphic (even cuspidal Maass, eigenvalue 1/4, level 1951, nebentypus χ); (ii) L(s,ρ̃) has infinitely many poles, all in Z(L(λ)) ∩ {0<σ<1}, and strong Artin fails. Hence: strong Artin for ρ̃ ⟺ every zero of Λ(λ) is a zero of Λ(μ) with at least its multiplicity — one censusable pair, no twist quantifier, no magnitudes.

*Proof.* If a pole exists, the trivial twist has a pole, so Booker's Theorem gives infinitely many, confined by polar rigidity; a cuspidal π would force entirety — so not automorphic. If no pole exists: no Dirichlet twist has a pole (else infinitude of poles of L(ρ̃) itself), all twisted Λ are entire order-1 ratios (Lemma 1/Brauer) with exact FEs; the Weil–Maass converse ((q,N)=1 twists suffice, per the paper) gives automorphy. ∎

## 2. Theorem B (halting detector; unconditional, magnitude-free)

There is an explicit procedure PD using only integer reads — dodged-box censuses enumerating Z(Λ(λ)) by height (workload ≤ N_λ(T), effective), then shrinking dodged micro-circles D around each λ-zero cluster reading w(D) = N_μ(D) − N_λ(D) ∈ ℤ, dovetailed over (cluster, radius, precision) — such that: **PD halts ⟺ ρ̃ is not automorphic**, and a halt outputs a certified pole (a read w(D) < 0 forces one).

*Proof sketch.* Soundness: the winding of Λ(ρ̃) = Λ(μ)/Λ(λ) on ∂D is Z − P; negative forces P > 0, so Theorem A(ii). ## 3. Theorem C (quantitative transfer; proof skeleton) and the one-witness reduction

Then L(s,ρ̃) **itself** has a pole of height ≤ T* = A·max{1+|τ₁|, (C(q)/r)²}·log²(same), with A, C(q) computable from the effective inventory and the displayed constants of Booker's identity.

*Skeleton.* Re-derive the identity without the finiteness hypothesis: the all-poles residue representation converges because horizontals can be dodged at heights T_k where the integrand is ≤ exp(C₃log²T_k)·e^{−cδT_k} → 0; a pole of L(ρ̃) at height γ enters with weight ≲ exp(C₄log²(qγ))·e^{−cδγ} (dodged residue caps — upper bounds only). If L(ρ̃) had no pole below G(δ) := (A/δ)log²(2+1/δ), the polar side is ≤ ∫_G^∞ κ log(Qγ)e^{−cδγ/2}dγ ≤ e^{−cδG/4} ≤ 1, since the A-margin makes cδγ/2 ≥ C₄log²(qγ) on γ ≥ G (bookkeeping checked). The witness signal Ω(r·δ^{−β₁}e^{−cδ|τ₁|}) beats the regular side (convexity constants only; vertical lines at σ = 2, −1 need no minimum-modulus input) once δ < δ* = c′·min{1/(1+|τ₁|), (r/C(q))^{1/β₁}}; β₁ ≥ 1/2 gives δ* ≥ c′·min{1/(1+|τ₁|), (r/C(q))²}. Contradiction; a pole lies below T* = G(δ*). ∎ (Status: skeleton-grade — the weight shape and dodged-horizontal steps are re-derived, exponents cited from the paper; no step uses a residue lower bound except the hypothesis r itself.)
