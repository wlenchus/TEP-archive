## 0. The problem, and why it continues the first charge

**Selberg's eigenvalue conjecture (1965, open):** for every congruence subgroup Γ ⊆ SL(2,ℤ), the smallest nonzero Laplace eigenvalue on Γ\H satisfies λ₁ ≥ 1/4. Equivalently: no exceptional Maass cusp forms — writing λ = 1/4 − δ², the claim is δ = 0. Status (S1/S3, verified): the record is **δ ≤ 7/64** (λ₁ ≥ 975/4096), Kim–Sarnak 2003, extended to all number fields by Blomer–Brumley 2011, **unimproved in 23 years**; Newton–Thorne's symmetric-power functoriality (2021–26) does not transfer to Maass forms (no Galois representations — the method needs algebraicity); the conjecture is *verified* computationally for Γ₁(N), N ≤ 880 (Booker–Lee–Strömbergsson 2020); one long-standing unestablished claimed proof exists (Unterberger, arXiv:2001.10956 — treated here as unestablished, per S3). This is harder and less obscure than Crouzeix by any measure: it is a limb of the Ramanujan complex, tied to Langlands functoriality, and stuck.

Why it is the continuation: the first charge established, at the matrix level, that *boundaries read interiors through passive ports at budget 2, the seam is where power dies, and the silver-vs-2 gap is an unclosed reflection channel*. Selberg is that statement on an arithmetic surface — and one rung below RH on the same ladder: the cusp of the modular surface is *literally* a scattering port whose reflection coefficient is a ratio of completed zeta functions; the seam is λ = 1/4, the exact boundary the corpus's Γ₀(229) form was certified on. **Crouzeix → Selberg → RH is one budget statement at three depths** (matrix numerical range → arithmetic surface → the port's own scattering coefficient), and this brief charges the middle rung.
