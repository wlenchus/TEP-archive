## **1. The reading (four steps, three of them theorem-grade)**

**1.1 The boundary carries an operator harmonic measure of total budget 2.** For any convex Ω ⊇ W(A), define on ∂Ω (arc length ds, outward normal ν): dM(σ) = (1/π) · Re[ ν(σ) · (σI − A)^{-1} ] ds. Then **dM ⪰ 0 pointwise and ∮ dM = 2I.** Proof in five lines, and every line is TEP: convexity puts Ω in the half-plane {Re((z−σ)ν̄) ≤ 0} at each σ, so W(σ−A) lies in a rotated right half-plane, so ν̄(σ)(σ−A) is *accretive*; the inverse of an accretive operator is accretive (Re⟨Bx,x⟩ ≥ 0 ⇒ Re⟨y, B⁻¹y⟩ ≥ 0 with y = Bx — the positive-real/impedance step, the exact move that makes DtN operators positive-real); hence the density is PSD. The Cauchy integral of the resolvent is I; taking real parts doubles it: **mass 2 — one unit per quadrature.** This is the Crouzeix–Palencia kernel lemma, re-derived as a passivity statement. [Machine-checked: PSD everywhere and mass 2I to 1e−5 across all 13 test matrices; script attached.]

**1.2 The Boundary Dilation Identity.** M/2 is a genuine POVM on ∂Ω, so Naimark dilates it to a spectral measure: there exists a **normal operator N with σ(N) ⊆ ∂Ω** on a larger space, with f(A) + g_f(A)* = 2 · P_H f(N)|*H for every f holomorphic on Ω, where g_f is the Cauchy transform of f̄ over ∂Ω (the reflected read — the anti-analytic quadrature).* ***Every operator is half of a boundary-normal pair, read through its numerical-range port.*** *Berger's classical unitary 2-dilation for the disk is the special case where the reflection dies (g = ā₀I; zero when f(0)=0 — verified in closed form: for A = [[0,2],[0,0]], f = z, the residue computation gives g ≡ 0 exactly). The C–P norm bound ‖f(A) + g_f(A)*‖ ≤ 2 sup*∂Ω|f| is now *one line*: ‖2Pf(N)‖ ≤ 2‖f(N)‖ = 2 sup|f|. The conjecture, restated: **the reflected channel can always be dropped at zero net cost.**

**1.3 The necessity lemma [T, exact].** Let c = ‖f(A)‖ with sup_∂Ω|f| = 1, x the maximizing unit vector, T = f(A) + g_f(A)*, and define the **interference functional** I(f,x) = Re⟨f(A)x, g_f(A)*x⟩. Then c² + I = Re⟨f(A)x, Tx⟩ ≤ ‖T‖·c ≤ 2c ⟹ **I ≤ c(2−c).** Three lines, no approximation. Consequence: **any Crouzeix violation (c > 2) requires destructive interference (I < 0) between the transmitted and reflected reads at the maximizer.** The conjecture is exactly the statement that destructive interference never beats the budget.

**1.4 The silver ratio is the price of treating the reflection adversarially.** Bound the g-channel by norm instead of controlling its sign and you get the bootstrap c² ≤ 2c + 1, whose positive root is **1+√2 — the silver equation x² = 2x + 1.** That is where the world's constant sits, and *why* it sits there: the method spends the whole budget and then pays a norm-ransom to an adversarial reflection. The conjectured 2 is the closed budget; the gap [2, 1+√2] is the unclosed reflection channel, priced.
