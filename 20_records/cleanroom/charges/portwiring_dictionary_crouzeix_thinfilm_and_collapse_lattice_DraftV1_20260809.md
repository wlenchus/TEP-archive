## 1. The wiring dictionary (what kind of budget each system keeps)

The two systems keep the same two-term budget `x² + u = 1` under different **port wirings**, and the difference is bookkeeping, not physics:

- **The film is interference-internalized.** Its three channels R, T, A exit distinct ports, so no cross-term appears in the energy budget — but the interference has not vanished; it lives *inside* R and T at the amplitude level. The exact reduction (PU §4) says so explicitly: `r = r₁₂(1−b)/(1−r₁₂²b)` is a coherent sum of bounce amplitudes — a Fabry–Pérot interferometer whose fringes are pre-summed before the budget is written.
- **The Crouzeix port is interference-exposed.** Its two coherent arms — the direct read `t = c/2` and the seam read `ρ = |f(z₀)|/2` — recombine at *one* port, so the cross-term appears explicitly: `x_p² = t² + ρ² + 2tρk` (Addendum 2 §2, verbatim: "the thin-film structure appearing *inside* the Crouzeix problem, not by analogy to it").

Conversion between the two bookkeepings = expanding or pre-summing coherent amplitude sums. The invariant content is the Gram structure of the amplitude set; which cross-terms are visible is a wiring choice. This is worth stating as a rule because Case 1's whole content is: **match configurations by wiring class, not by channel names.**

**[id] The Berger read has the Salisbury wiring.** `T = 2Pf(U)P` (Addendum 2 §1): in through the compression, a *unitary* bounce, out through the compression — a lossy element traversed twice with a lossless reflector behind it. That is the Salisbury screen's wiring (film · spacer · perfect mirror), with the dilation constant 2 as the amplitude scale and passivity supplied by unitarity. Will's disk-normalization point lands exactly here: **the "mapping of the defined class to the disk" is the unitary dilation itself** — von Neumann/Berger *is* the normalization-as-unitarity-constraint, and the disk is this family's terminal port (a(Ω) = 0 end of the MMOR family; cf. TF I.5's terminal-d=2 posture). The triviality worry inverts: the disk case is not where the framework is vacuous; it is the collapsed/terminal face from which the a(Ω) > 0 family un-collapses.

**[id] The two reflection-kill mechanisms, named in both languages.** The dictionary's §3 reading of the 2 vs 1+√2 gap — *closing* the seam port (ρ → 0) versus *anti-phasing* it (k = −1, ρ > 0) — is, in optics, the distinction between **anti-reflection matching** (no reflected amplitude exists) and **critical coupling** (a reflected amplitude exists and is destructively cancelled). Ransford's Q4.1 — does unitality force the constant 2 — becomes: *can an admissible read reach the anti-phased (critically-coupled) configuration of a port it cannot close?* The frontier question of the charge is a wiring question.
